01 — The Pulse

Growth is still here. The easy growth is not.

For five years the EV story was a single exponential line. In 2026 that line bends. Global passenger EV sales reach roughly 23.3 million units, an 11% increase over 2025 — a record year in absolute terms, and the slowest percentage growth of the modern EV era.

Both things are true at once, and most commentary picks only one. The volume is enormous: EVs are now close to three in every ten new cars sold on Earth. But the market has moved past the phase where any new model at any price found a buyer. Growth now has to be earned on cost, charging convenience and product — not on incentives.

Global passenger EV sales, millions of units. 2026 is a record — at a third of the growth rate of 2023.

The one-line version: 2026 is the year the EV transition stopped being a technology story and became an economics story.
02 — Three Markets, Not One

The global EV market has split in three.

Averages now hide more than they reveal. China, Europe and North America are on genuinely divergent trajectories — one mature and competitive, one regulation-driven and accelerating, one in policy-induced contraction.

Monthly EV sales growth, year-on-year, early 2026. The same month, three different markets.

🇨🇳

China

≈64% of domestic car sales are EVs

The world's first market where electric is the default, not the alternative. China is the only major market offering more electric models than combustion ones. Growth has slowed as purchase-support schemes normalise, but the structural position is won: domestic price competition is brutal, margins are thin, and exports to Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America are the new growth engine.

Mature · price-led · exporting

🇪🇺

Europe

+24% y/y · 17.4% BEV share in 2025

The strongest grower of 2026. CO₂ fleet targets do the work that subsidies used to, pushing automakers to actually sell the electric cars they build. Chinese entrants — tariffs notwithstanding — are reshaping the affordable segment, and European OEMs have finally answered with sub-€25k models. Charging build-out under AFIR is the binding constraint, not demand.

Accelerating · regulation-driven

🇺🇸

North America

−33% y/y in January 2026

The outlier. The $7,500 federal credit expired 30 September 2025, pulling a record quarter of demand forward and leaving a hole behind it. January 2026 was the weakest EV month since early 2022. Forecasters expect roughly a 15% full-year decline in US BEV volume and share slipping toward 8%. State incentives and OEM discounting are cushioning, not fixing, it.

Contracting · policy shock

🌏

Rest of World

+92% y/y — the fastest growth anywhere

The quiet headline of 2026. Southeast Asia, India, Brazil and Mexico are growing off a small base at rates nobody else can match, largely on affordable Chinese imports and locally assembled models. Small numbers today; the demand curve for the 2030s.

Emerging · import-led

03 — The Players

Two giants, trading the crown.

BYD ended 2025 as the world's largest EV maker on total new-energy volume, powered by price cuts, a wide model ladder and fast-growing exports. Tesla reclaimed the pure-BEV lead in Q1 2026 on a quarter that still disappointed its own analysts — a useful signal of how compressed the top of the market has become.

Beneath them, the interesting movement is structural: legacy OEMs are trimming EV targets in North America while holding them in Europe, and a second tier of Chinese brands is scaling faster internationally than either giant did at the same stage.

  • BYD — volume leader on NEV sales; export-led growth into Europe, LatAm, ASEAN.
  • Tesla — retook the global BEV crown in Q1 2026; defending margin over volume.
  • Legacy OEMs — a two-speed strategy, split by region and regulation.
  • Chinese challengers — the fastest-scaling cohort in the industry.

04 — Batteries

LFP won the decade. Solid-state is still a promise.

The battery pack is roughly a third of an EV's cost, so battery chemistry is really a pricing strategy. In 2026 the industry has settled on its answer.

LFP share of the global EV battery market. Cheap, cobalt-free, and now close to half of everything shipped.

Lithium iron phosphate has climbed from roughly 41% of the market to around 47%, and is on track for close to half of North American demand by 2030. LFP packs averaged more than 40% cheaper per kWh than nickel-manganese-cobalt alternatives in 2025, and they contain no cobalt or nickel — which means they sidestep exactly the commodity shocks that broke supply-chain planning twice this decade.

Solid-state remains a 2027+ story. 2026 is the verification year: prototype trials, vehicle installation testing, small pilot lines from Toyota, CATL, BYD and QuantumScape. Expect small-batch premium applications in 2027–28 and genuinely affordable mass-market solid-state only after 2030. Anyone selling you a shorter timeline is selling you something.

05 — Charging

The bottleneck moved from the car to the plug.

Charging infrastructure is now a $37.4 billion market in 2026, forecast to reach $136 billion by 2033 — a ~20% compound growth rate. More importantly, the mix is shifting: a rising share of new installations are fast and ultra-fast units rather than slow AC posts.

North America is consolidating on NACS. Ford, GM, Hyundai and most others have committed, networks are retrofitting, and new models ship with the port natively. CCS and adapters will persist for years, but the standards war is effectively over.

In Europe, AFIR mandates set minimum coverage along core corridors. In China, density is already past the point where range anxiety shapes purchase decisions — which is a large part of why its adoption curve looks the way it does.

06 — Outlook

What to actually watch.

01

Does the US find a floor?

The post-credit trough should bottom in 2026. Whether share recovers toward 10% in 2027 or stalls near 8% is the single biggest swing factor in global forecasts.

02

Sub-$25k, profitably

Affordable EVs exist. Affordable EVs that make money mostly don't. LFP plus scale is the only credible path, and 2026–27 is when it gets tested.

03

Chinese exports vs. tariffs

Europe, LatAm and ASEAN are absorbing Chinese volume fast. How trade policy responds sets the affordable-segment price floor worldwide.

04

Grid, not cars

Fast-charging demand is becoming a distribution-network problem. Interconnection queues, not charger hardware, are the emerging constraint.

05

The PHEV detour

Plug-in hybrids keep outgrowing expectations in China and Europe. A bridge, or a decade-long plateau? The answer reshapes battery demand forecasts.

06

ICE's long decline

Combustion vehicle sales have peaked and are in structural decline. Road-transport oil demand is at or near its peak. That direction is not in dispute.

The bottom line

2026 is not the year the EV transition stalled — it's the year it stopped being subsidised into existence. A market selling 23 million cars with 11% growth, falling battery costs and a settled charging standard is not a market in trouble. It's a market that now has to compete on merit, and it is mostly winning.
